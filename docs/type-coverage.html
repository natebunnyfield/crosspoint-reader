What the catalogue can set, and what it can't

The YAML files everything under four headings — Serif, Sans-serif, Monospace, Dyslexia, Accessibility. That is a filing system, not a classification. Sorted by what the letterforms actually are, the 37 families land in 13 of 22 structural classes — nine are empty. The fourth group below, engineered-for-a-condition, is a cross-cutting axis rather than a shape, so its four classes are counted separately and all four are occupied.

Two things the filing hides

Alegreya is filed under Sans-serif. It is a serif — Huerta Tipográfica's calligraphic bookface, and its own description in the YAML says "calligraphic serif/display". It sits in the sans block between IBM Plex Sans and the Monospace header. Nothing breaks, but any count of "how many sans do we have" made from the headings is off by one.

The S tier is better spread than the catalogue. The six installed families occupy six different classes — Caslon, Venetian, Garalde, Clarendon/legibility, humanist sans, grotesque. The catalogue as a whole is not: 19 of its 25 serifs are oldstyle or contemporary-text, two classes. Cutting Archivo and Host Grotesk improved the spread rather than narrowing it, because all three grotesques were one cell.
